## Hypothesis

(`2dca52ce`'s lesson, applied). §10's claim is about the *conversion*, so the
right site is wherever an `Error` stops being a failure — which is `classify`
and nowhere else. `Actor::answer` builds the abstention but is handed a
`Classified`, not an `Error`; and the other thing reaching it was never an
`Error` at all, since `hard_cap` drops a merely-late answer and already logs.
So one line at the conversion covers all three callers and double-logs nothing.

The deeper expectation, which held: a section whose only listed gap closes in
ten lines has more wrong with it than the gap says, because the gap is what the
auditor could *see*. §10 had three more things missing; §2 had one.

What the campaign is really evidence for is that **the gap list under-reports a
section as often as it over-reports one**. Three campaigns of findings say the
list is stale and the code is the oracle, and that stayed true — one of my two
assigned gaps was closed before I arrived. But the *other* section, whose one
gap closed in ten lines, had four more claims with nothing behind them, and
none of the four was in the list. The cheap move after closing an assigned gap
is to close the campaign; the paying move is to read the section's remaining
sentences one at a time, which produced four of the five commits.

The one thing to be careful about is `CHANGE-core-023`, because it is the shape
the audit cannot catch. It edits a document toward code it did not write, it
closes a *minor* rather than a gap, and so it moves no number at all — which is
stated in the changelog entry rather than left for a reader to work out.
