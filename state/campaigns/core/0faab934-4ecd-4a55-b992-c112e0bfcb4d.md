## Target

`core.md#the-trait[93f2f340e6]` — the handler's search surface on
`ProjectView`. It is the section's only open gap, so closing it is a section
going clean rather than a gap going away.

The claim: "handlers do their own reads, parses and searches through
`ProjectView` — read, parse and scan are all on it — and every path a handler
holds came from `ProjectView::candidates` or `::lookup`". `read` and `lookup`
exist; `candidates`, `parse` and `scan` do not, so a handler cannot search at
all and `ProjectPath`'s unforgeability protects a scope nothing can reach.

The three are one piece of work, not three: `scan` takes what `candidates`
returned, `parse` takes what `read` returned, and the point of the claim is
that a handler's whole route to the world outside its document is this one
object. Closing any two would leave the section with a gap and the number
unmoved.

## Hypothesis

The gap's stated blocker — "the parse LRU and bounded pool the claim routes
through have no entry point" — is not one. `conformance-005` already ruled
that `ProjectView` gets no read cache (no corpus, no benchmark, and a cache
behind a `Sync` `&Query` is a lock), and the same reasoning covers the parse
LRU: the slow simple version is a fresh parse, and the pool is an optimisation
`CLAUDE.md` forbids until the corpus asks for it. What is left after removing
both is ordinary code.

Expected to surface, from reading `resolution.md` §3 beside §1.3: `parse`'s
grammar has no source in the printed signature (Class B), and
`scan(&self, req) -> ScanOutcome` cannot express a deadline abort without
reporting a partial scan, which §4 forbids (Class A).

## Outcome

**Confirmed.** `read`, `parse`, `scan`, `candidates` and `lookup` are all on
`ProjectView`, committed, and covered by tests that fail if a method is
removed rather than by reading the file.

The hypothesis held exactly: neither the parse LRU nor the bounded pool was
load-bearing. `conformance-005` had already settled the identical question one
level down, and what remained after dropping both was ~350 lines of ordinary
code. **This is the reusable result** — an audit gap that names an absent
subsystem as its blocker is worth re-reading against the decision records
before believing it.

Two spec defects fell out:

* **CHANGE-conformance-011** (Class A). §3 prints
  `scan(&self, req) -> ScanOutcome`, which cannot be written: `read` fails on
  an expired deadline and §4 deleted every way to report a partial scan, so
  the expiry has nowhere to go but the `Err`. Now `Result<ScanOutcome, Error>`.
* **conformance-012** (Class B, open). §3's `parse(&self, path, text)` has no
  route to a `tree_sitter::Language` — the signature predates `ProjectView`
  leaving `driver`, which held the registry. Provisionally the grammar is a
  constructor parameter; one production site tagged.

Deliberately not done, and not gaps: `DefinitionHints` (needs `regex`, and §9
makes it a phase-3 extraction), the parse LRU, the bounded pool, and `rayon`.
`deps.md` §14 has each dependency arrive with its first user, and a sequential
exhaustive scan has no user for `rayon` until there is a benchmark.
