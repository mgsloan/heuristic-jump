## Outcome

Confirmed, for all three targets and the three extensions. Six sections
should go clean; two minors and one false-positive check bug are fixed
beside them.

The hypothesis held in the shape it was written: two of the three were the
document being stale and one was the code being wrong, and in each case
what decided it was evidence outside both. For `#campaigns-are-the-unit`
that evidence was the intervention log — the several-targets rule is a
human's prompt revision with an argued commit message, so the document was
never updated to match a ruling that had already been made. Preferring the
prompt there is not the spec-toward-code move §7 warns about, and the
changelog entry says so with the commit named.

The half that did not resolve is the one the hypothesis predicted would
not: whether a loop's prompt may state §7's five forms of progress. That
is `harness-013`, open, with the text left in place as the reversible
side.

Two things are worth carrying past the diff. First, `prompt_body_problems`
had already established that a prompt property can be checked in
*template* coordinates, which is what makes the leak check immune to a
journal entry or a findings digest naming a check — those are a loop's own
prose and failing a gate over them would punish the honesty §13 asks for.
Second, deriving the step names from `harness/gate` means a step added
tomorrow is covered the day it is added; the convention that a name
without a hyphen is a leak only as `hj <name>` is what keeps the English
word "crates" from failing every prompt in the tree.

What this campaign did **not** take, deliberately: both remaining gaps in
`harness/loop` — the gate between the rebase and the fast-forward
`[02927bbcaa]`, and the worker that audits `[3530d46864]`. They share
their reading with each other and not with this campaign, and more
importantly the close path cannot be exercised without performing a real
merge into `main`. That wants a scratch-repository fixture first, and it
is a campaign of its own.
